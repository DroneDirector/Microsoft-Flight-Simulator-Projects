**Taking staff to court**
- Serious conduct reports (a skipped safety check, an aircraft hit with a vehicle, maintenance signed off without the paperwork, checklist shortcuts, tools left on the apron, reckless driving on the apron, thrown passengers' bags) can be reported to the police: from your office after talking to them, straight from the office's list of reports, or from the report itself. The person is suspended and the prosecutor charges them.
- Day one: you give evidence from the witness box. The report and their record are shown as exhibits, and the prosecutor, the defence and the judge question you. Telling it as it is helps the case; exaggerating is usually found out.
- Day two: the defendant takes the stand and you put your own questions: an open question, the evidence, or pressing hard. They answer as the person they are (honest, defensive, dismissive or lying); confronting a liar with the evidence catches them out. A colleague is heard, both sides close, and you make your statement as the injured party: leniency, a fair decision or the full penalty, and the cost of any damage.
- The verdict and the sentence are read out in court. If you claimed damages, they are paid to you.
- The papers follow the trial: each day in court and the verdict.
- Lies can come out, though not often. Something untrue you tell the court may be caught there and then, or leak after the verdict even if the judge believed you: the papers have it, your company rating falls, a wrongful conviction is overturned and the employee compensated, and you are charged with perjury. Then it is your turn in the defendant's chair: the transcript of your evidence, the records against it, the judge's questions and your plea. Settings → Court → "Lies in court come out" sets how likely that is: never, rarely (the standard), sometimes or often.

**Your office**
- When there are open conduct reports, "Conduct reports" in the office lists them with who, what and when. Invite someone in: you sit at your desk, there is a knock, and they walk in and sit facing you.

**Cabin**
- Wide-body cabins have overhead bins over the middle seats too, and every row placard sits flat along a bin's edge instead of floating in the air.
- Inside the aircraft (cabin, walking, window seats) the flight shows as one slim line along the bottom (route and progress, phase, altitude, speed, distance and time left, the passenger list and loadsheet) instead of the large card.
