Weather limits per aircraft type (src/core/weather.ts, WX_LIMITS)
==================================================================

What a hired pilot may take off and land in. Beyond these, the flight waits at the gate (cancelled after
five hours and flown the next day), holds before landing, reroutes around a hazard area, or diverts.

Crosswind values are the manufacturers' maximum demonstrated crosswind (or the flight crew training
manual guidance where that is lower), including gusts. D = not confirmed from a published source yet.

Airport limits (by airport size in the app): big airports CAT III (75 m), regional airports CAT I
(550 m, 200 ft), small airports a non-precision approach (1,500 m, 400 ft). The higher of the aircraft's
and the airport's minimum applies.

For every aircraft and airport:
- No take-off or landing with a thunderstorm over the airport (TS in the METAR). Aircraft not approved
  for IFR also avoid thunderstorms in the vicinity (VCTS). A passing storm at the destination of a flight
  over 600 nm doesn't hold the departure (it will have moved on).
- Wind or gusts of 50 kt or more: no movements.
- Heavy freezing rain or drizzle: no movements (de-icing fluid holdover times too short). Any freezing
  rain: aircraft without known-icing approval wait.
- Heavy snow or blowing snow: departures wait for runway clearing and de-icing.
- Tropical cyclone SIGMET over or within 150 nm of the airport: the flight is cancelled.
- Volcanic ash SIGMET over the airport: waits; on the route: reroute (25 to 45 minutes extra).
- Thunderstorm SIGMET on the route: reroute (8 to 25 minutes); severe turbulence: level change.
- Severe icing SIGMET on the route: aircraft without known-icing approval wait.
- Thunderstorms or fog in the destination's TAF (TEMPO or PROB): holding 15 to 35 minutes, or about
  three times in ten a diversion to the nearest airport with good weather and a usable runway; the
  flight carries on to its destination afterwards.
Only weather happening now counts: legs caught up after the app was closed are not judged by today's weather.

Data: aviationweather.gov (US National Weather Service, public domain): METAR, TAF, international and US SIGMETs.
