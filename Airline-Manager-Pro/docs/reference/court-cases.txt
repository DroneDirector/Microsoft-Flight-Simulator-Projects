Court cases: real prosecutions the case types follow (src/core/court.ts, src/core/court-plus.ts)
=================================================================================================
People, airlines and newspapers in the app are made up; these real cases shaped the kinds of offences,
charges and sentences.

- Flying a whole flight over the alcohol limit: three crew members of a US airline were convicted in
  1990 after flying a scheduled flight drunk (Northwest Airlines Flight 650); prison sentences of about
  a year to 16 months, licences revoked.
- Undeclared dangerous goods: a maintenance contractor was convicted after oxygen generators were loaded
  as cargo with wrong paperwork (ValuJet 592, 1996); most of the conviction was overturned on appeal.
- A pressurisation error after maintenance: airline managers were tried for manslaughter after a
  pressurisation switch was left in the wrong position and the crew lost consciousness (Helios 522, 2005).
- Managers and controllers convicted of negligent manslaughter after a mid-air collision (Überlingen,
  2002) and a runway collision (Linate, 2001).
- An airline and a mechanic convicted after debris on a runway caused a crash (Concorde, 2000); overturned
  on appeal in 2012.
- Deliberate acts that cost lives: in real cases the perpetrator usually died; here the case goes to trial.

Court systems: a jury in the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, Ireland and New Zealand;
a professional judge with lay judges in the Nordic countries, Germany and Austria; a panel of professional
judges elsewhere. Release after about two thirds of a prison sentence (85% in the United States), with
time in custody before the trial deducted.
