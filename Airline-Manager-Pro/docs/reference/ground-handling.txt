Ground handling: your own ground crew at your bases (src/core/ground.ts)
=========================================================================

Your hub and every other airport where you base aircraft need your own ground crew. At every other
airport the local handling company turns your aircraft round for a fee.

Own ramp team on one turnaround (loaders for bags and cargo, push-back/tug driver, ramp lead; fuel and
catering are the airport's companies):
  GA (C172, PC-12)      0   the pilot handles it
  Bush (Beaver, 208...) 1
  Turboprop (ATR 72)    3
  Regional (CRJ)        4
  Narrow-body (A320/737) 6
  Wide-body (777/787/A350/747) 10
Other aircraft being turned round at the same base at the same time need their own team. With fewer
people than the team, the turnaround takes longer (at most two hours more); with nobody, the aircraft
can't leave. Off shift counts as available (called in); off sick, on leave or on strike doesn't.

Staff a base supports per based aircraft (two shifts, days off and leave): GA 1, bush 2, turboprop 6,
regional 8, narrow-body 12, wide-body 22, about 15% more at big airports, plus 2.

Background (C = worked out from these, to be refined):
- A narrow-body turnaround involves up to about 30 people from about six organisations (ramp, baggage,
  cleaning, catering, fuel, maintenance): aerocorner.com/blog/airline-aircraft-turnaround/
- Air Canada at Heathrow (wide-body only): 105 ramp, 117 baggage, 37 cabin services, 16 ground support
  equipment, 8 weight and balance, 21 maintenance: hse.gov.uk/airtransport/case-studies/aircanada.htm
