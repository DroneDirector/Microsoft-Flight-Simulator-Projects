FLAPS, LANDING GEAR AND PHASE SPEEDS
Reference guide | Prepared 30 October 2026
Companion to takeoff-speeds-and-weights.txt, landing-speeds-and-weights.txt and
additional-aircraft-speeds-and-weights.txt

SCOPE AND SAFETY NOTE
For every aircraft in Airline Manager Pro: the flap settings with their limit speeds, the landing gear
limits, the normal take-off and landing settings, the speeds at which crews normally select each flap
step on the approach, and the climb, cruise and descent speeds. These are broad, approximate
educational figures for the app's simulation, not operational speeds. Never use this guide to operate
an aircraft.

HOW TO READ THE LIST
- VFE = the highest speed a flap setting may be used at (knots, indicated airspeed).
- Gear: VLO extend / VLO retract = the highest speed to lower / raise the gear; VLE = the highest speed
  with the gear down. "Fixed" = the gear doesn't retract.
- Selected at = the speed at which the setting is normally selected on the approach at a typical
  landing weight (Airbus: Green Dot, S and F speeds; Boeing: flap manoeuvring speeds from Vref).
- Clean = typical clean manoeuvring speed. VMO/MMO (VNE/VNO for light aircraft) = maximum speeds.
- Status: C = limit speeds as published in the manufacturers' operating documents and the type
  certificate data sheets, entered from those published values and still to be confirmed line by line
  against the current EASA/FAA type certificate data sheet; E = estimated from crew practice (selection
  speeds, climb and descent speeds). Selection speeds are always E: they depend on the weight.
- How the app uses them: after take-off the aircraft flies V2 + 10 to about 1,000 ft, then accelerates
  and brings the flaps in step by step (each one about 10 kt above its selection speed), gear up with a
  positive climb, clean by 3,000 ft (light aircraft bring the take-off flaps in by about 400 ft). On
  the approach it slows through the steps at the "selected at" speeds, gear down at about 2,000 ft above
  the ground, in the landing setting by 1,000 ft.

  3A12 Cessna 172).
